The career possibility space

There is no default job. There is a field of real destinations.

If you are weighing a humanities major — or halfway through one and wondering what happens after graduation — this site was built for you. It maps humanities degree records with career trajectories attached: what people who started where you are actually did over the following decades, counted honestly and mapped as a possibility space you can stand inside.

View the destination field as a table

One rule governs everything here: what clears the evidence bar is shown; what doesn't is reported, not filled in. Computed numbers are badged green. The short narrative glosses written by NHA staff are badged amber, and there are no invented career stories anywhere on this site.

Mobility styles: early movers vs stayers, measured

pipeline data

Maybe your real worry isn't the first job but the churn after it. This panel groups graduates by how often they changed jobs in their first three years, then measures where each group stood at year five. Mover groups are self-selected — descriptions, never causal claims.

Also measured, and worth knowing: early movers do not “settle” later — their later moves stay faster than stayers'. What changes is the character of the movement: more of it is job-to-job (fewer moves through a gap), and it covers more occupational ground. Movement is a style, not a phase — and neither style pays a seniority penalty here.

Seniority understates self-employed and portfolio careers, common in the arts. No wage data is used anywhere.

Honesty commitments

✓Aggregate only. No individual profile is ever displayed or reconstructible. Cells below the minimum support threshold are suppressed, and the suppression is visible in the interface.
✓This is not a population sample. LinkedIn skews professional, English-speaking, and recently active. We label cohorts and never imply representativeness — the product frames possibility, not averages.
✓Survivorship is named on the chart. Long careers appear only if their owners stayed visible. Every long-view surface carries this caveat in place, not in a footnote.
✓Gaps are unknowns. A gap may be caregiving, travel, study, or a missing entry. The interface renders “gap (unknown)” — never “unemployed.”
✓No wage claims. The data has no salaries. Outcomes are trajectory proxies — seniority, preparation level, transition mix — and are labeled as such.
✓No causal claims about choices. Grad school, double majors, internships, and every other decision on the Choices view are described as associations among self-selected groups — never as effects of the choice.
✓Pre-committed comparisons. Where majors are compared, the comparison design is fixed in advance and we publish whatever the controlled comparison shows.

The year-10 cohort funnel

pipeline data

Only about one in five qualifying degree records carries a usable graduation year, so equal-window cohorts are far smaller than raw record counts. This is the dominant constraint on every per-major number, and it is reported here rather than hidden. Graduation anchors use the two validated tiers — an observed end year (A1), or start year plus a fitted duration (A2, ≥80% of held-out gold within ±1 year); a third inferred tier failed validation and is excluded.
