The career possibility space

Here is what two million real careers say is actually reachable.

The debate about humanities majors runs on starting-salary tables and anecdotes. Pathways runs on 240,277 humanities degree records with complete career trajectories attached — what people actually did over the following decades, mapped as a possibility space a student can stand inside.

Careers sort into 27 real communities of movement — and only 56% of that structure lines up with the official occupation taxonomy. Nearly half of observed career movement cuts across the categories that standard salary comparisons are built on. Honesty commitments

✓Aggregate only. No individual profile is ever displayed or reconstructible. Cells below the minimum support threshold are suppressed, and the suppression is visible in the interface.
✓This is not a population sample. LinkedIn skews professional, English-speaking, and recently active. We label cohorts and never imply representativeness — the product frames possibility, not averages.
✓Survivorship is named on the chart. Long careers appear only if their owners stayed visible. Every long-view surface carries this caveat in place, not in a footnote.
✓Gaps are unknowns. A gap may be caregiving, travel, study, or a missing entry. The interface renders “gap (unknown)” — never “unemployed.”
✓No wage claims. The data has no salaries. Outcomes are trajectory proxies — seniority, preparation level, transition mix — and are labeled as such.
✓Pre-committed comparisons. Where majors are compared, the comparison design is fixed in advance and we publish whatever the controlled comparison shows.

What's illustrative in this prototype, and when it becomes real

illustrative

The dataset, the transition network, the seniority measurement, and the humanities classification above are live and validated. The per-major panels (destination fans, named pathways, long-view curves, pillar scores) are illustrative placeholders: they show the product shape while the education-to-career join — linking each degree record to its owner's trajectory — is built on the same keys that already exist on both sides. Nothing in the design depends on data we don't have.
