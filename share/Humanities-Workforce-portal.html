The career possibility space

Here is what two million real careers say is actually reachable.

The debate about humanities majors runs on starting-salary tables and anecdotes. Humanities Workforce runs on 240,277 humanities degree records with career trajectories attached — what people actually did over the following decades, mapped as a possibility space a student can stand inside.

Honesty commitments

✓Aggregate only. No individual profile is ever displayed or reconstructible. Cells below the minimum support threshold are suppressed, and the suppression is visible in the interface.
✓This is not a population sample. LinkedIn skews professional, English-speaking, and recently active. We label cohorts and never imply representativeness — the product frames possibility, not averages.
✓Survivorship is named on the chart. Long careers appear only if their owners stayed visible. Every long-view surface carries this caveat in place, not in a footnote.
✓Gaps are unknowns. A gap may be caregiving, travel, study, or a missing entry. The interface renders “gap (unknown)” — never “unemployed.”
✓No wage claims. The data has no salaries. Outcomes are trajectory proxies — seniority, preparation level, transition mix — and are labeled as such.
✓Pre-committed comparisons. Where majors are compared, the comparison design is fixed in advance and we publish whatever the controlled comparison shows.

The year-10 cohort funnel

pipeline data

Only about one in five qualifying degree records carries a usable graduation year, so equal-window cohorts are far smaller than raw record counts. This is the dominant constraint on every per-major number, and it is reported here rather than hidden. Graduation anchors use the two validated tiers — an observed end year (A1), or start year plus a fitted duration (A2, ≥80% of held-out gold within ±1 year); a third inferred tier failed validation and is excluded.

What's computed, and what's still editorial

pipeline data

Every per-major number on this site — cohort funnels, destination fans, sectors reached, long-view curves, and pillar scores — is computed from the committed data spine under equal observation windows: a year-N statistic includes only people whose graduation anchor is at least N years before the snapshot, and any cell supported by fewer than 40 people is suppressed. Occupation classification pools two sources: the deterministic O*NET lexicon backbone (6-digit; always wins) and a calibrated LLM jury at major-group grain (two-model unanimity with reference-title anchors, 88.7% agreement with held-out deterministic gold, pre-committed gate 0.85; strings where the two models disagree or abstain ship nothing). A relative-risk bias check compares deterministic-only and pooled fans before pooled numbers ship; each fan reports its unclassified share as its own honest bucket and its source blend. The pathway routes are counted occupation-group transitions over fully classified consecutive years. Two things remain editorial and are badged amber wherever they appear: the named role-level routes in Stories and the narrative glosses. The four pillar scores are computed, but the pillar framework itself is a framing device, not a validated measure.
