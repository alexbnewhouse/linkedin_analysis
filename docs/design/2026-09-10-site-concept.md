# The student site: concept

**10 September 2026.** What the undergraduate-facing site is, who it is for, what it argues,
and what it refuses to be. Backed out of the user journey rather than written ahead of it, so
every claim here is one the journey actually delivers.

It stands on `FOUNDATION.md` Part 3, which named the site and set its audience, and on
`docs/MESSAGES.md`, which fixed the eight things NHA wants to say. It extends both in three
places, marked where they occur. The journey itself is
`2026-09-10-user-journey-narrative.md` in prose and `2026-09-10-user-journey-plain.md` as a
chart; the build-level version is `2026-09-10-user-journey.md`.

---

## 1. The site in one sentence

`FOUNDATION.md` §3.1 already has it, and it holds:

> A place where a humanities undergraduate can see the actual range of work that people with
> their degree went into, follow specific routes into the ones that interest them, and come
> away understanding that the open-endedness of that picture is normal rather than a warning.

The journey adds one clause that the original sentence does not carry, and it turns out to be
load-bearing: **and, starting from a job they are already curious about, see who is already
doing it and what those people studied.**

That third movement is not decoration. Without it the site only works for a reader who has an
interest but no destination, which describes a sophomore and not a senior. With it, the same
instrument serves a reader who arrives already saying "I want to work in publishing", and
turns a named job into a question the corpus can answer rather than a dead end. How often
students actually arrive that way is a question for the think-aloud round, not one this
document should assert.

**The job to be done, unchanged, in the student's words:** is there anything out there for
me, and can you show me something specific enough that I believe you.

---

## 2. Who it is for

Undergraduates first. Their advisors second, mostly as the person the student brings a page
to. Nobody else.

Provosts, legislators, trustees and journalists are served by the written report
(`2026-09-01-report-outline.md`), and the site links to it rather than trying to be it. That
split is the most important decision in `FOUNDATION.md` and nothing in the journey work
disturbs it. The honesty apparatus that makes a sceptic trust us reads to an anxious
sophomore as hedging, and the reverse is equally true.

One amendment from the service-design pass. Four kinds of person will arrive: a student
alone, a student in an advisor's office with the advisor driving, a whole class with an
assignment, and someone who does not believe the premise — a parent, or a faculty member who
reads "humanities careers" as a euphemism. They get one address and one canvas. Context
arrives in the link, never as a choice on the page, because the person who self-identifies is
usually not the person reading. The parent picks "student". The sceptical faculty member
picks nothing and closes the tab.

## 3. The core concept

**One canvas with two ends, not a sequence of screens.**

Everything else follows from this. The reader sets a starting point, a destination, or
neither, or both. Which of the three questions they are asking is a consequence of what they
have filled in:

| Starting point | Destination | The question they are asking |
|---|---|---|
| — | — | Is there anything out there at all |
| set | — | Where could I go |
| set | set | How would I get there |
| — | set | Who is already doing it |

Nobody has to learn this, and no screen names it. The reader only ever picks something or
clears it. But it is what lets them cross between the three questions without going back to
the beginning, and it is why anything named on the screen — a job, an employer, a major, a
certificate — can be clicked and become the new starting point.

**The consequence worth stating plainly: there is no results page.** A reader who follows
their curiosity three hops out never hits a dead end and never has to press back. This is the
difference between a place to explore and a report with tabs, and it is the single thing that
most distinguishes this from every tool in section 5.

### What this changes

`FOUNDATION.md` §3.2 describes the site as nine screens in sequence, the first four being a
spine. **That sequence does not survive, and the change needs NHA sign-off.**

A sequence answers "where could I go" and then stops. A student who arrives already knowing
they want to work in publishing has to sit through an introduction to get nowhere.

What survives, and it is most of the work: the nine screens become the content shown inside a
destination, in the order §3.2 already puts them. What the work involves, then how people got
in, then what they studied, then what they carried, then where it happened. The order was
right. It was the framing of it as a route through the site rather than as the contents of
one stop that does not hold.

## 4. What the reader is promised, and what they leave with

The promise is narrow and it is deliverable: we will show you what people with your degree
actually did, at a level of specificity you can check, and we will tell you how many people
each answer rests on.

We do not promise to tell them what to do, what they are good at, what they are, or what
anything pays.

The test of the site is the last step of the journey, not the first. A reader who leaves with
a feeling has been entertained. A reader who leaves with a certificate they could start now,
a graduate step and who took it, employers in their state, and a page to bring to an advisor
has been helped. At least one of those carries a real date, and the date comes from the
provider or the programme, never from this sample.

That page is the product's actual output. It has five downstream readers who will never be in
one room — the student in three months, the advisor before an appointment, an instructor
marking an assignment, a sceptical parent, and whoever receives the questions the student was
given to ask. It is specified in `2026-09-10-user-journey.md` §6.

## 5. Why nothing else does this

From the twenty-two-tool survey (`docs/notes/2026-09-08-career-tools-survey.md`), which found
four families and no overlap with what this site does:

- **Occupation-first government tools** (O*NET, CareerOneStop, BLS Career Exploration)
  describe jobs well and never connect them to what anyone studied.
- **Major-first outcome tools** (BLS Field of Degree, Hamilton Project, NY Fed, Georgetown,
  Humanities Indicators, PSEO, Discover Uni) show where people with a major ended up as one
  cross-sectional snapshot, and nearly all lead with earnings.
- **Profile-data tools** (Lightcast, Steppingblocks, Emsi) are the same data class as ours.
  They can show sequences, but they are sold to one institution at a time, sit behind a
  login, and are not built to make a field-level argument.
- **Editorial tools** (What Can I Do With This Major, Roadtrip Nation) are what career centres
  actually hand students, and they contain no outcome data at all.

The gap, stated as the survey states it: no public tool shows, for a named field of study and
across institutions, how the same people moved from first job to year five to year ten at
occupation grain, with the empty cells explained.

Two of those clauses are the whole differentiation. **The same people over time** is what
separates this from every cross-sectional snapshot, including the ones with far better
sampling than ours. **With the empty cells explained** is what separates it from the profile-
data vendors, who have comparable data and no reason to show you where it thins out.

We are not competing on sample quality, and we should never claim to. ACS beats us on
representativeness and always will. We are the only ones who can show the shape of a
humanities career over a decade at role grain with education attached.

## 6. What the site argues, mapped onto the journey

`docs/MESSAGES.md` fixes eight messages and routes each to the report, the site, or both.
Laying them against the journey steps is new here, and it surfaces two problems worth
knowing about before design starts.

| # | Message | Where it lands in the journey | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opens a possibility space, not a pipeline | Steps 2 and 5. The whole field, then the guess and the reveal | Ready. The site leads with it |
| 2 | Careers unfold over time | Step 9, in the elapsed years | Needs a build: the linearity typology |
| 3 | Reaches unexpected destinations | Step 6, and the rare-jobs tail | Ready, needs reframing |
| 4 | Transferable skills enable moves | Step 9, as what they carried | **Asserted, not measured.** See below |
| 5 | Chart a course, stay open to what arrives | Step 7, the same data read backwards | Built, unwired |
| 6 | Paths converge in leadership | **Nowhere.** Report only, descriptive form only | Correctly absent |
| 7 | Coursework translates to careers | Step 9, as what they studied | Built, unwired |
| 8 | Concrete career pathways | **Step 13, the exit** | **Blocked on the time-axis decision** |

**The first problem is message 4.** There is no skills field in this corpus. LinkedIn's skills
and endorsements section was not captured, and every proxy is a title, a self-written
description, or a certification — a title-inferred skill is collinear with the move it is
meant to explain. The journey shows certificates people held, which is a real and checkable
thing, and it is not a skills claim. The site should stop short of saying skills transfer and
show what people carried instead. The report can make the moves argument properly.

**The second problem is worse, because of where it lands.** Message 8 is the one that reaches
the reader at the exit — the concrete credential, the graduate step, the employers — and it is
the step the whole site is tested on. It is blocked on the career-entry axis decision
(`FOUNDATION.md` §3.5, decision 2), which is still open. The most important step in the
journey depends on a question nobody has answered.

## 7. Honesty is a feature of this product, not friction in it

A concept-level claim, and an expansion on prior documents, which treat suppression as a
constraint to be managed.

Roughly 98.5% of readers land on a group large enough to describe. The 1.5% who do not are
the moment the site is most likely to be believed, because it is where it visibly declines to
say something it could have gotten away with saying.

So the thin case is a designed step in the journey rather than an error state. The screen
never goes blank, gives the reason — that ten people cannot be described without describing
individuals — shows the wider answer, names exactly which condition was set aside to get it,
and offers to put that condition back and widen a different one instead. The grammatical
subject of those sentences is never the reader.

The same logic governs the rare jobs. 86.4% of the 638,368 job titles in the corpus were held
by exactly one person, and work under those singleton titles accounts for 29.2% of all the
working life we observe. The tail is not an edge case to tolerate; it is most of what is out
there, and 13,933 titles clear the reporting bar. A student who finds one job on that list
they had never heard of has had the experience the whole site is for.

The rule that follows: a thin route is described as rare and real, never hedged and never
dressed up. Fourteen people in this sample did this is a complete, honest, encouraging
sentence. Deficit framing is banned the way wage claims are.

## 8. What the site refuses to be

Carried from `FOUNDATION.md` §3.4 unchanged, with three additions the journey work produced.

Unchanged:

- Any salary, earnings, or ROI figure
- Any demographic breakdown
- Any individual person, profile, or verbatim quotation
- Any prediction, recommendation engine, or jobs-you-might-like result
- Any single-answer quiz or assigned personality type
- The internal archetype vocabulary, anywhere on screen, in any language
- Provost-facing and legislator-facing framing

Added:

- **No account, and no dashboard.** State lives in the address bar, on the device, and on the
  printed page. A login wall at the moment of curiosity is where these tools die, and a
  dashboard promises a relationship the site cannot hold.
- **No advisor-facing activity feed.** The advisor portal receives only what a student chose
  to send. A tool that reports on students to staff is a surveillance product, and it costs
  the appointment the candour that makes it worth thirty minutes.
- **Not a job board.** Named employers are places where people worked, not openings.

On wages specifically, the refusal is a door rather than a silence. When a reader asks what
this work pays — and they will — the site says what it does not record, hands over the federal
occupation code and the Bureau of Labor Statistics series that does measure pay, declines to
rank occupations, and returns the reader to the exact view they left. Refusing silently reads
as evasion. Answering would be a lie.

## 9. What has to be true

**Decisions still open, carried forward from `FOUNDATION.md` §3.5.** All three were listed as
needed before design starts, and this document does not resolve any of them.

1. **The canonical cohort.** Which of the five humanities definitions the site counts. It
   changes numbers already shown to people.
2. **The time axis.** Career entry as primary, with graduation as a labelled sensitivity
   check, is the standing recommendation. Message 8 and therefore the exit step are blocked
   on it.
3. **Report and site are two products.** The journey assumes yes throughout.

**And one new one from this document:** §3 replaces the nine-screen sequence with one canvas.
That is a change to an approved plan and should be said out loud rather than absorbed.

**Writing, still on the critical path.** The privacy answer, without which career services
cannot use the site at all. The population statement. Every empty state, written before the
screen that contains it. Add to that list the eleven first-person descriptions of kinds of
work, which the journey puts at the second step and which do not exist yet.

Reading down the journey's own last column, the site needs about two dozen short pieces of
writing, of which six carry the argument: the opening claim and population statement
together, the eleven descriptions, the gloss on the guess, the three widening sentences, the
line that makes a rare job read as real, and what each parting choice meant. None of them are
captions, and no dataset writes them.

## 10. How we would know it worked

Not traffic, and not time on page — a reader who leaves in forty seconds having read one true
sentence has been served.

Three things, in increasing order of how hard they are to observe:

1. **A reader can name a specific job they had not heard of before.** The cheapest to
   deliver, the most likely to be shared, and the one the tail exists for.
2. **A reader leaves with something that has a date on it.** A certificate enrolment, a
   programme deadline, or a booked appointment. This is the difference between being
   entertained and being helped.
3. **An advisor's thirty-minute appointment starts at the second question instead of the
   first**, because the student walked in with a page. That is the outcome career services
   actually values, and it is the one that would make the site get assigned rather than
   merely bookmarked.

The honest counter-test, worth writing down now while it costs nothing to admit: if the site
is used mainly by students who were already going to be fine, we will have built a
reassurance instrument for the reassured. Nothing in this corpus can tell us whether that is
happening, because the people it would be failing are the same people it systematically
undersamples.
