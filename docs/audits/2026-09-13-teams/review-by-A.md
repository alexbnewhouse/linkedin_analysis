## 4. Missed by all three

1. No complementary-cell suppression exists, and the journey's filter and ladder design invites differencing. Suppression is per cell only: portal/analyses.py:73 drops a fan cell when n < MIN_SUPPORT and analyses.py:144 drops a role when n < DETAIL_MIN_SUPPORT; a grep for complementary or secondary suppression across portal/, archetypes/, cohorts/ finds nothing (VERIFIED). The journey shows the wider cell beside the narrower one (LAD2, LAD3, CHOICE, rule 7.9 "selecting a state re-states the denominator"), offers binary and small-domain like-me filters (institution control has three values, NCRED and NGEO are filter-only), and STIPPLE and TAILSTATE print "the number of people inside the suppressed region". With a three-valued filter, showing "English x Legal = 173" and "English x Legal x public = 165" discloses private = 8 without ever rendering a below-bar cell; with one suppressed title, the STIPPLE count is that title's count. Recommendation: apply secondary suppression at the cube-build stage (suppress the complement when a filter has a small domain and exactly one value is below the bar, and emit STIPPLE counts only when at least two cells are suppressed), and write it into rule 7.8. Effort S in code once the cube exists; the design rule is the larger change.

2. The unclassified half of every year-10 cohort is two different things, and the journey's FIELD promise ("This is what they were doing at year ten. All of it") has to decide how to render both. On C's population at year 10 (reproduced), 8,938 of 42,813 windowed persons (20.9%) have no step active that year at all and 11,503 (26.9%) have a step with no occupation group. The pipeline's own rule forbids labelling the first group (portal/common.py:10 "Gaps are unknown, never inferred unemployment"), and the LIMITS copy says time out of work is not observed, which is only half true: an absence in the panel is observed, its meaning is not. None of the three reports says which of the two the site should treat as a rendered category and which as a denominator exclusion, and C3's two-denominator claim object needs a third count (n_no_step) to be honest. Effort S in data; a wording decision for NHA.

3. The launchboard.py:42-44 comment ("~95% of post-bachelor grad rows carry a usable start_year") that B listed as unverifiable is resolvable and matters for ACT2: among grad-level education rows with an end_year, 154,570 of 154,570 carry a start_year (100%), and among persons in the portal membership 99,709 of 99,709; among all grad-level rows only 177,114 of 736,264 (24.1%, B's figure) do (VERIFIED). So the grad-track denominator is right for the rows it counts, but three quarters of graduate degrees in the corpus are undated and fall out of "the career year they took it" (ACT2) entirely. The panel should carry an undated-grad count next to the dated one.
