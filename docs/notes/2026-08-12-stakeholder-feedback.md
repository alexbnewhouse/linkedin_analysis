Reactions from colleagues (including the NHA director) to the two review surfaces:
`share/Humanities-Workforce-portal.html` (the built portal) and `prototype/atlas.html`

[`nha-career-skill-archetypes-framework.md`](nha-career-skill-archetypes-framework.md).

## The framing note that reorganizes the rest

> **N8 — Three distinct questions are being conflated.** "We are having issues
> distinguishing between the three distinct questions we need to answer. Those are: **What
> can we do with the data? What do we want to argue? How do we want to show it?** We need
> additional research and qualitative thought on all three. This effort in the portal was
> designed specifically to answer #3, but I think we need to back up and trace the
> connections between all three questions."

**Altitude, clarified 2026-08-12:** this is meant at the **highest project level** — what is
this project actually doing, and what are we producing? Not a portal-design question. Q3 is
"what is the deliverable," not "which chart goes where"; Q1 is the corpus's capability
envelope as a research asset, not which panels are buildable; Q2 is NHA's position, not
portal copy.

Read the rest of this log through it: everything else is downstream of a deliverable that
has never been named.

## Cohesion and audience

**N1 — Cohesion across portal and archetype narratives.**
The two surfaces may not be communicating the right thing at the top. The narratives do not
yet cohere as one argument.

**N1a — The NHA director gets distracted by the fake data in the atlas.**
Placeholder counts pull review attention onto the numbers instead of the structure.

**N1b — Archetypes need to be defined specifically against the real data.**
The archetype definitions in front of reviewers are vocabulary, not evidence. They need to
be grounded in what the corpus actually shows for each one.

## Theme and framing

**N2 — Movement and pathways are *a* theme, not *the only* theme,** and may not even be the
main theme to stress. This includes movement *between* archetypes.

**N2a — Archetypes are a way *in* to particular roles, not a way to hold onto or define a
person's identity.** The taxonomy is a door, not a label.

**N2b — The idea of the archetypes is to show ways into a career path; we need to link
skills language to actions.**

**N3 — The director's framework** (source doc, see transcription) proposes a four-dimension
model — Archetype × Career Pathway × Sector × Work Context — with nine skill archetypes
written in humanities-coursework language, and explicit guidance that sectors are filters,
not destinations.

## Content gaps

**N4 — Nothing anywhere says what skills students get from their coursework.**

## What is working

**N5 — Colleagues like the "choose your own path" section** toward the bottom of the portal.

**N6b — Positive reaction to the idea of clustering people across industries based on the
type of work they do,** and good signal in specific clusters such as "creatives."

## What is not working

**N6 — Some clusters stretch too broadly right now.** (Same note as N6b: the cross-industry
idea is right, the current bucket boundaries are not.)

## Affect and impact

**N7 — Distinguish between our arguments-via-research and the affective impact of engaging
with the portal.** Feelings we want to impart:

1. **Confidence** — that a user can find a path toward their personal version of "success."
2. **Engagement and interest** — in particular types of paths/roles/careers similar to a
   person's own interests.
3. **Empowerment** — for career-services staff, in communicating the validity of the
   humanities for career success.
4. **Understanding of long-term adaptability and resilience** over time.
5. **Better understanding among advocates that careers are non-linear** — iterative,
   sometimes lateral or even backwards in the short term in exchange for long-term change
   and transformation.
