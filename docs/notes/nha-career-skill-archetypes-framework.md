# The NHA Career Skill Archetypes Framework (director's draft)

**Provenance.** Authored by the NHA director in a brainstorming session with an AI
assistant; delivered 2026-08-12 as `Career Skill Archetypes Framework.pdf` (12 pp.) at
the repo root. Transcribed here verbatim-in-substance so it is greppable, linkable, and
diffable. **This is a source document, not a project decision** — it is a normative
career-services taxonomy, not a data-fitted one. What we do with it is argued in
`NARRATIVE_FRAMING_PLAN.md` §3.

The framework's own structural claim, and the reason it matters to us: it is
**four orthogonal dimensions**, not one list.

```
Job Title → Primary Archetype → Secondary Archetype (optional) → Career Pathway
          → Fundamental Orientation → Signature Skills → Sector(s) → Work Context
```

---

## The nine archetypes

Each carries a **fundamental orientation** written in the first person (usable verbatim
as a self-identification prompt) and a **signature skills** list written in
humanities-coursework language.

### 1. Communicators & Creators
> I turn ideas into messages, stories, and experiences that engage an audience.

rhetorical analysis; audience awareness; argument construction; storytelling;
interpretation; synthesis; translating complex ideas for different audiences; oral
presentation; media and visual literacy; creative ideation; content strategy.

## Sector (a filter, not a destination)

> Sector and work context are separate classification dimensions. They should not compete
> with skill archetypes: a role can use the same core skills in many different industries
> and at different levels of responsibility.

| Sector | Typical settings |
|---|---|
| Business & Professional Services | Consulting firms, agencies, corporations, professional associations, B2B services |
| Technology | Software, digital products, IT, platforms, AI, technology services |
| Healthcare & Human Services | Hospitals, health systems, clinics, behavioral health, social services, community health |
| Education & Academia | K-12, colleges and universities, research centers, education providers, edtech |
| Government & Public Policy | Federal, state, local government, legislative offices, public agencies, think tanks |
| Nonprofit & Philanthropy | Nonprofits, foundations, advocacy organizations, associations, NGOs |
| Arts, Culture & Media | Museums, libraries, archives, publishers, journalism, film, performing arts, cultural organizations |
| Finance & Financial Services | Banks, investment firms, insurance, accounting, fintech, financial institutions |
| Law & Legal Services | Law firms, legal departments, courts, legal aid, regulatory and compliance organizations |
| Retail, Hospitality & Tourism | Retailers, hotels, restaurants, travel, tourism, visitor attractions, customer-facing services |

## Work context

| Work context | Definition |
|---|---|
| Individual Contributor | Primarily responsible for one's own specialized work rather than formally managing a team. |
| Manager | Leads people, projects, programs, or functions and is accountable for team-level outcomes. |
| Executive | Sets organizational or enterprise-level direction and carries broad decision-making responsibility. |
| Founder / Entrepreneur | Creates and leads a new venture, organization, practice, or business. |
| Freelancer / Independent Practitioner | Works independently for clients or maintains a self-directed professional practice. |

## Classification guidance (verbatim in substance)

1. **Use the archetype to describe the work, not the industry.** A healthcare
   communications director belongs primarily under Communicators & Creators; healthcare is
   the sector. A nonprofit program manager belongs under Leaders & Organizers; nonprofit is
   the sector.
2. **Allow a secondary archetype when the role is genuinely hybrid.** A museum curator may
   be primarily a Researcher & Educator and secondarily a Communicator & Creator. A
   nonprofit executive director may be primarily a Leader & Organizer and secondarily a
   Connector & Relationship Builder.
3. **Do not force every title into a single meaning.** The same title can represent
   different work in different organizations. Classify based on the role's actual
   responsibilities and dominant skills, not the title alone.
4. **Keep pathways more stable than job titles.** Job titles change rapidly. Career
   pathways such as Fundraising & Development, Applied Research & Insights, or Project
   Management provide a more durable organizing structure.
5. **Treat sectors as filters, not destinations.** The framework is most useful when people
   can see that a skill archetype travels across sectors. Researchers, communicators,
   analysts, relationship builders, and organizers are needed in nearly every part of the
   economy.

## Coverage note (ours, not the director's)

The framework has **no home for line frontline service work** — food service, retail floor,
warehouse, personal service — except obliquely via Helpers → Hospitality & Guest Experience
(which is written for guest-experience *management*, not line roles). In our measured panel
that work is 2.4% of humanities person-years (Hospitality, Retail & Service) and is
concentrated at career-years 1–3. Any adoption has to decide explicitly what happens to it;
see `NARRATIVE_FRAMING_PLAN.md` §3.4.
